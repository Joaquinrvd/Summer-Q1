

 1. Conceptualize a Social Enterprise

My business idea is about helping people who are lonely, bored, or stressed from running out of time. A lot of people don’t have someone to hang out with, and sometimes they just need a friend — even if it’s temporary or just to have some fun. I want to create something like a service where people can connect with others to either hang out, play games, go outside, or even help with stuff like grabbing dinner or doing small errands.

This could help with loneliness and also support people who are always rushing around and don’t have time to do everything. I’ve seen people who just want someone to talk to or need help before a game night or event. Even just having someone to hang out with could make people feel better and lead to real friendships.

 2. Strategize Legal Structure

I think the best legal structure for this kind of business would be something simple to start with, like an LLC (Limited Liability Company), because it gives flexibility but also protects me legally. Later, I could look into something like a B-Corp (Benefit Corporation) since the goal is not just to make money but also to help people in a real way.

The reason I’d start with an LLC is because it’s easier to manage in the beginning while I figure everything out. But since this business is focused on improving people’s lives — helping with loneliness, boredom, and everyday stress — I’d want a legal structure that supports both the social side and the business side. A B-Corp could be perfect in the future once things grow.
